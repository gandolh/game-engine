MateQuest is the fourth game on the shared engine, and the one that isn't a
watch-it-play sim: an **educational math roguelike** aligned to the Romanian
national curriculum (*programa școlară*), where **solving a curriculum problem
_is_ the combat action**.

## The pillar: the solve *is* the swing

Most math games bolt a math widget next to a game, and children engage only with
the wrapper. The north star here is Raizada's principle — *solving the problem
and the fun action must be the same act* — so math is never a toll gate you clear
to reach the game (the Prodigy failure mode).

In a Pokémon-style battle you pick **Attack / Heal / Shield**, and a curriculum
problem for that action appears. **Answering correctly is what makes the action
land**, with damage/heal/shield scaled by difficulty and speed; a wrong answer
*fizzles*. Add Slay-the-Spire stakes — enemies telegraph intent and strike on
their turn, there's a real HP bar, enough wrong answers is death — and the math
carries genuine, low-anxiety tension.

Difficulty *is* grade, and the player chooses it: the branching node map
bifurcates into easy and hard paths, where the hard branch means tougher math and
better loot. An incentive to climb, not a forced climb. The theme is Romanian
folklore — a Făt-Frumos-style hero trio against **Zmeu**, **Balaur** and **Muma
Pădurii**.

## Scope, stated honestly

<Aside type="caution" title="Grades I–IV — and not all of that band">
  The design was drawn against the curriculum's full **I–VIII** ladder. The game
  implements the **primary band only** (`Grade = 1 | 2 | 3 | 4`), and every
  document that said "I–VIII" was describing an intention rather than the build —
  narrowed to match the code on 2026-09-19.

  Within I–IV it covers **addition, subtraction, multiplication and comparison**,
  with ranges widening per rung and multiplication withheld from grade I (the
  *programa* introduces it in clasa a II-a, and the generator throws if dispatched
  there). **Division, intro fractions, units of measure and intuitive geometry are
  in that curriculum row and none of them is implemented.** "Grades I–IV" is
  honest about the *rung*, not a claim of full coverage within it.
</Aside>

V–VIII was narrowed away rather than built, and the reason is a design finding
worth keeping: it is not "the same with bigger numbers". Fractions and negatives
need **answer-input affordances that don't exist** — plausibly a larger job than
the generators — and a multi-step equation may not fit a few-seconds-under-turn-
pressure loop at all. The bar is also higher here than elsewhere in this repo: in
this game **a wrong answer is a wrong thing taught to a child**, so every topic
needs an *independent* answer check, and the test helper now fails if one is
missing.

## How it runs

Like Citadel and Hollow, MateQuest runs its sim in an **in-browser Web Worker**
and needs no server — it ships as a static site a school can host. Problem
generators are **deterministic**, driven by the seeded `Rng`, which is what makes
curriculum content unit-testable: the same seed yields the same problem set, so a
generator's output can be asserted rather than eyeballed.

The UI defaults to Romanian, with a bilingual RO/EN toggle from day one behind
centralized i18n. The palette is **Resurrect-64** (`MATE_PAL.*`) — the fourth
palette scope in the repo, validated by the same per-path guard test that holds
EDG32 and Apollo-46 in place.
